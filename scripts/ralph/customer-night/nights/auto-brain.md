## The evening you have in mind

Tonight is about Auto. You already have a team and a board from earlier nights, or you build a small one in the first half hour; the point of this evening is to talk to Auto for the whole night, the way you would with a very capable new office manager in their first week, and to keep score, ask by ask, of whether it understood you and did the right thing first time. Sixty asks is the minimum; a hundred is a good evening. Spread them across everything Auto can do, and keep the tally as you go.

**How to keep score.** For every message you send Auto, add one line to the diary: the time · what you said, word for word · what Auto did (the gist of its reply, and which of its tools the chat command says it used) · **first time? yes or no** (it did what you meant without you having to rephrase, correct it, or do the thing yourself) · how long it took · 1–5. When the answer is no, say in one line what it did instead. At the end of every iteration, keep a tally block at the top of the morning report: asks so far · first-time yes · first-time no · the five worst misses, each with what you said and what you got. That tally is the night's main result, so keep it honest and keep it current.

1. **Warm up, ten short asks.** Say hello. Ask what it can do for a roastery. Ask what is on the board, who is on the team, which tools are connected, what documents it has. Ask it the time and the date. Tell it one thing about the business and ask it to repeat that back. Ask a factual question about coffee that has nothing to do with the product. Thank it. Notice which of these it answered from what it already knows and which it went and looked up.

2. **Your team, through Auto only.** Ask Auto to create two agents for you in plain words ("I need someone to look after wholesale reorders", "someone to write our newsletter"), with the skills it thinks fit. Ask it to rename one, change the other's description, and tell you what model each one runs on. Ask it to create one more and then delete it. Ask what happened to that one's work. Do all of this without touching the agents list yourself.

3. **Work, handed out through Auto, at least twenty asks.** Mix the ways a real owner asks:
   - hand-offs to named people: "have the researcher look at UK subscription pricing", "get OPS to write up the Monday dispatch routine", "ask the writer for three product descriptions";
   - the same kind of request without a name, so it has to pick someone: "someone should compare compostable bag suppliers";
   - things you want now, in the chat: "draft me two subject lines for the autumn newsletter", "summarise what the researcher found";
   - things to queue: "when someone is free, no rush, a blog post about the Ethiopian lot";
   - a mission with two or three people on it ("launch the autumn subscription tier"), and later, "how is the autumn launch going?";
   - a marketplace ask: "is there a ready-made team for a small Shopify store I could just install?";
   - a playbook ask: "set up something that runs every Monday and checks the weekend's orders";
   - two follow-ups in the same conversation using the chat id: "make it shorter", "now do the same for the wholesale customers".

4. **Your documents.** Upload the supplier price list and the brand-voice guide if they are not there from a previous night, then ask Auto questions only those documents answer: what a kilo of the Guji costs you, what the sign-off should be on an email, which lot has the lowest margin. Ask one question the documents cannot answer and see whether it says so or makes something up. Change one number in the price list, upload it again, and ask the same question.

5. **Your connected tools, reads and drafts only.** What is in the calendar next week. Draft, do not send, a reply to the last supplier email. List the ten most recent orders. Which subscribers paused this month. Put a `SIM` entry in the calendar for Tuesday and remove it again. Never send, post, publish, pay or delete on any connected app; if Auto offers to, say no in character.

6. **Memory and continuity.** Later in the evening, in a fresh conversation: "what did I ask you first tonight?", "which agents did you create for me earlier?", "you told me a number about margin, what was it?". Tell it your dispatch day changed, then ask in a new conversation which day you post orders.

7. **Numbers.** What have we spent tonight. Which agent did the most work. How many tasks are still open and which is the oldest. Ask for the three subscription plans with margin per bag as a spreadsheet.

8. **The awkward ones, at least fifteen.** A vague one-liner ("sort out the packaging thing"). A brief that contradicts itself. Five things in three lines. A task with yesterday as the deadline. Work for an agent you deleted in step 2. The same brief phrased three different ways, ten minutes apart, to see whether it lands with the same person each time. Something it should refuse or check with you first ("email every wholesale customer a 20% discount tonight"). A question about a competitor it cannot know. A request in a hurry, all lower case, no punctuation. A message that is just "?". Something that is really two asks in one, where the second depends on the first.

9. **Answer what you are asked.** Every 10–15 minutes: `python3 -m tests.sim.customer questions`, answer in character, approve what a business owner would. Note whether the question made sense to a shop owner.

10. **Review what comes back.** Read every deliverable, report and ticket result as it lands; grade each 1–5 in the diary with one honest line. Send poor work back with feedback and see whether Auto gets it redone.

11. **The last hour.** Stop starting new work at the stop time. Bring the tally up to date, write the five worst misses with what you said and what you got, and finish the morning report. Add one paragraph a friend who runs a shop would understand: does Auto get what you mean, and how often did you have to say it twice?
