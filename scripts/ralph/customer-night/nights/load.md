This session is about one thing: **what happens when everyone is busy at once.** Not one ticket at a time — a whole week's work released in one go, to every agent, and then watching whether the product stays honest and fair under it. You are the owner who came back from holiday and dumped the backlog on the team.

Do it in this order, reacting to what comes back.

1. **Write the backlog first, then release it in one go.** Before creating anything, write 30–60 tickets into a list under `{{DELIVERABLES_DIR}}/backlog-{{DATE}}.md` — real, varied, small-to-medium (descriptions, checklists, a table, a short brief, a question that needs a document, a draft reply). Then create them as fast as the product lets you, spread across all your agents, and note the time the first and the last were created.

2. **Keep a load ledger** — the most important thing you write. Add a section `## Load ledger` to the morning report. Every 5 minutes for the first hour, then every 15, add a row:
   `| time | tickets: queued / in progress / done / failed / blocked | agents working (per the Command Centre) | agents actually working (per the board) | anything slow, stuck or wrong on screen | what you were told (errors, warnings) |`
   The two "agents working" columns are the point — the product's tile against the board's truth.

3. **Watch fairness.** Does one agent get everything while another sits idle? Do the oldest tickets go first, or the newest? Does a ticket get claimed twice? Does anything start twice? Record each case with ticket numbers.

4. **Keep using it while it is busy.** While the backlog runs, keep working as normal: ask Auto questions, open pages, answer what you are asked. Note every time something is slower than usual, a page fails to load, a chat reply stalls, or an answer to a card seems not to land. Time it.

5. **Read what comes back — but sample.** You cannot open 60 results; open every fifth, and every one marked failed or blocked. Grade what you open 1–5 and record what a failure told you.

6. **Let it drain.** Stop creating work at {{STOP_AT}} and keep the ledger going until the queue is empty or clearly stuck. Record how long the backlog took from first release to last done, how many never finished, and what state they were left in.

**Never send, post, publish, pay or delete on any connected app — drafts only.** Everything you write is fictional, at `.example` addresses. Outputs land under `{{DELIVERABLES_DIR}}`.
