This session is about one thing: **can you hand this product a routine and trust it to run it?** Every week has jobs that repeat — Monday's dispatch checks, the green-coffee reorder, the fortnightly newsletter, chasing cafés that have not paid. Automatos calls a saved routine a **Playbook**. You are going to get some, make some, run them, schedule one, and break one on purpose, and find out whether you would leave your Mondays to it.

Do it in this order, reacting to what comes back.

1. **See what exists.** Ask Auto what playbooks you have and what it could set up for a business like yours. Browse the marketplace for playbooks; install **two** that fit a coffee roaster (or the closest thing you can find). Note how long it takes and whether it was obvious what each one will do before you run it.

2. **Keep a playbook ledger** — the most important thing you write. Add a section `## Playbook ledger` to the morning report, one row per playbook run:
   `| # | playbook | how it came to exist (marketplace / Auto made it / you built it) | what you asked it to do | steps | did every step run? | time per step | what came out (open it) | right / wrong / partial | what a failure told you |`
   Aim for **at least 10 runs** across at least four playbooks by the end.

3. **Have Auto make two from your words.** Describe routines the way you would to a new manager, e.g. *"every Monday morning, check which green coffees are under 30 kg and draft — don't send — a reorder email to the importer for each"*, and *"after each roast day, write the roast log up as a one-page summary in our brand voice"*. Read what it built before running it: are the steps what you meant? Change one step and see whether the change sticks.

4. **Run them with real inputs.** Run each playbook at least twice — once plainly, once with a different input (a different lot, a different week). Open every output. Where a step hands work to an agent, note whether the agent got everything it needed or was left guessing (a step that reaches an agent with a blank or a literal `{input}` in it is a failure worth recording word for word).

5. **Make one need you.** Run a playbook where a step genuinely needs a decision from you (which importer? how many kilos?). Does it stop and ask you? Where does the question show up, and does the run carry on after you answer?

6. **Schedule one — and take it down.** Schedule one playbook to run once, about 30 minutes from now. Check it ran when it said it would, and what it produced. **Then remove the schedule** — nothing you schedule may keep running after this session.

7. **Break one on purpose.** Run a playbook in a way that must fail — point it at a document you do not have, give it an importer that does not exist, or ask for something no step can do. Record exactly what you were told: did it fail loudly, name the step and say what to fix? Or did it report success over nothing?

8. **Keep going.** Runs take minutes, sessions take 10–30; never sit waiting. Answer what you are asked (`python3 -m tests.sim.customer questions`) every 10–15 minutes. **Never send, post, publish, pay or delete on any connected app — drafts only.** Stop *starting* new work at {{STOP_AT}}; use the remaining time to finish the ledger, open every output, and write the report.
