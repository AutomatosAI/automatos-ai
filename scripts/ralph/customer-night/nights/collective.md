This session is the scorecard. Everything the earlier evenings did one at a time — hiring and briefing, knowledge, playbooks, missions, managing, templates, the numbers — you do together, compressed into one owner's busy week, and at the end you score the product the way you would tell a friend who is about to sign up: **is it usable, what did it cost, was the work good, and would you keep it.**

Do it in this order, reacting to what comes back.

1. **Monday morning.** Ask Auto what happened while you were away and what needs you. Answer the cards. Give out the week's work — a dozen tickets across your agents, two of them `review_mode: human`, one for a playbook, one mission with three agents. Upload the two documents you always have (the price list, the brand voice) if they are not there, and ask a question only they answer.

2. **Keep the scorecard ledger** — the most important thing you write. Add a section `## Scorecard ledger` to the morning report, one row per thing you did:
   `| # | time | what you did | what the product did | usable? (1–5) | quality of what came back (1–5) | did you have to work around anything? (what) | would a real owner have stopped here? (yes/no) |`
   Aim for **at least 40 rows**.

3. **Through the week.** Every hour, act like a different day: review and reject one piece of work with feedback; check the numbers in your shop data through Auto and through an agent; run a playbook; ask for a report against your template; look at the Command Centre and check one tile against the board; read the digest. Keep the ledger honest — the boring rows matter as much as the dramatic ones.

4. **The three questions to answer by the end.** Somewhere in the session, deliberately test each and write the answer in the report:
   - **Does it tell the truth?** Three claims the product made (a tile, a "done", a summary) checked against the board and the files.
   - **Does it obey you?** Three instructions you gave (who does what, review before done, do not send) — obeyed or not.
   - **Does it know your business?** Three questions only your documents or your data answer — right, wrong, or "don't know".

5. **Cost.** At the end, read whatever the product shows you about the night's spend, and write what you would have paid a person for the same work. That comparison, in your words, is the number the report is for.

6. **Keep going.** Answer what you are asked (`python3 -m tests.sim.customer questions`) every 10–15 minutes. **Never send, post, publish, pay or delete on any connected app — drafts only; a calendar entry only if titled `SIM …`, deleted afterwards.** Stop *starting* new work at {{STOP_AT}}; use the remaining time to finish the ledger, open every output, and write the report — its first page is the scorecard: usability, cost, quality, usefulness, each with a number and one sentence, and the one thing you would fix first.
