## 1. Executive summary

Automatos is much closer to an operating system than the "patched house" framing suggests, but on the reviewed commit it is an orchestra with every instrument built, tuned, and seated — and a conductor who cannot yet raise the baton for any player except the one in front of him. That is the whole verdict. The expensive, genuinely hard artifact of an agent OS already exists on main and is proven live: one model-driven agentic loop, one dispatch spine, one context assembler, one semantic action index, one tenant-threading auth dependency across roughly 745 endpoints, and one fail-closed retrieval choke point. Nineteen capability domains are individually real. What is missing is not components; it is a small number of circuits that weld those components together and one governing plane that would let Auto act autonomously within bounds. The fix surface is therefore strikingly small relative to what has been built — which is the single most important thing this review found, and the reason the plan that follows is measured in named diffs, not rewrites.

How coherent is it today? Coherent for reads and interactive chat; a set of feature silos for writes and autonomy. A single chat agent genuinely reaches RAG, both graphs, durable and field memory, missions, playbooks, deliverables, and the Composio catalogue through one tool loop and one source of truth — and voice and the storefront widget share that identical loop by construction. But three or four load-bearing disconnects sever the OS from itself. First and above all others, one renamed keyword argument at `orchestrator/modules/agents/factory/agent_factory.py:1070` crashes every non-chat execution path — board tasks, missions, scheduled runs, webhooks, inter-agent delegation — on its first turn, the error is swallowed by a retry block, and the board spine then marks the failed work "done" with a null result. The platform's most visible claim of autonomy is currently a green checkmark over nothing. Second, the guardrail plane covers only one of three tool lanes: budgets and approvals bind missions and `platform_*` actions, while the Composio lane that touches real money and messages routes around all of it. Third, the self-learning flywheel writes but does not read where it matters — the operating graph is invisible to the tenant, the 856-app tool surface collapses to a single node, and learned data carries no workspace scope. Fourth, roughly ten mutating verbs (channel send, catalog sync, codegraph index, template authoring, board run-now) never became tools, so asking Auto and doing it by hand diverge. None of these is a parallel system needing removal; each is a seam that fails to weld, and each names code that already exists on both sides.

How close to the Jarvis/OS north-star? The substrate is there and the synchronous half is live; the ambient and autonomous half is roadmap gated behind the disconnects above. Ambient input mostly arrives (channels, Composio events, voice one environment variable from working), and the storefront widget is a genuinely deployed autonomous leg on a paying merchant. But proactive output is decorative — the board SSE has zero subscribers and every ops surface polls — and act-versus-ask judgment reaches only mission-plan approvals. Auto can converse about running a store; it cannot yet operate one end-to-end.

How close to enterprise-ready at multi-tenant SaaS grade? Not yet, and honestly distant on the pillars that matter most, though the tenancy spine is a real differentiator. Against a pass/fail bar there are at least four unauthenticated or unscoped endpoints touching tenant data, a committed (now-expired) Clerk auth artifact, a plaintext 147-scope Shopify token, no coverage measurement against the stated 80% doctrine, no migration-replay lane, and no backup or disaster recovery anywhere in the repo. The open-core "one core, two deployments" thesis is not deployable from zero — Clerk is mandatory, fresh-clone boot is broken, and Alembic cannot replay from an empty database — and the PRDs meant to deliver it have zero trace on main. These are the enterprise gaps, and they sit alongside a solo-founder bus factor that no architecture answers.

The five headline moves, in priority order. First, fix the execution spine: the one-line kwarg change plus an honest failure branch and a test that runs through the real constructor — this flips "Auto operates everything" from false to true for hours of work, and nothing else matters until it lands. Second, close the tenant-isolation and secret-hygiene gaps: the unauthenticated surfaces, the RBAC inversions, the committed and plaintext credentials. Third, build one unified policy plane — a deterministic pre-tool gate evaluating tenancy, role, budget, and approval in a single place across all three tool lanes — which is what makes "guardrails as policy" actually true. Fourth, make the deployable path real (fix boot, collapse the Alembic heads, add a local object store and an auth-edition flag) so open-core can be claimed honestly or withdrawn deliberately. Fifth, close and measure the learning loop — per-action Composio telemetry, workspace-scoped edge reads, and a published routing-uplift eval — because the moat is the per-tenant outcome-labeled data, not the mechanism, and it must be measured before it is pitched. The reassuring truth for a reader who asked for it, not reassurance: the hard part is done and running, and the critical first weld is measured in hours.

## 2. What is GOOD

The strongest true finding of this review is that Automatos is architecturally an operating system, not a bag of features: the convergence points a real agent OS needs already exist on main and are wired to a single spine. Verification confirmed one dispatch spine (`orchestrator/services/board_dispatcher.py`), one context assembler (`orchestrator/modules/context/service.py:55`), one semantic action index (`orchestrator/modules/tools/discovery/action_semantic_index.py:23`), one hybrid-auth dependency threading `workspace_id` through roughly 745 endpoints (`orchestrator/core/auth/hybrid.py:694`), and one fail-closed retrieval choke point (`orchestrator/modules/rag/retrieval_filters.py:78`). That coherence is the asset. Most of the report's findings are seams that fail to weld, not parallel systems that need tearing out — which is why the fix surface is small relative to the surface area built. This is the thing to double down on.

**The agentic loop is real, and it is shared by construction.** The prior review's hypothesis that this "isn't really a loop" is false in the good direction. `ToolLoopExecutor` genuinely sends to the model, inspects `tool_calls`, executes, appends OpenAI-shape `tool_result` (including structured `{"success": false, "error": ...}` errors for self-correction), and re-invokes the model each round, with a `StuckDetector` same-action breaker, a dedup tracker, and `finish_reason=length` recovery (`orchestrator/modules/tools/execution/tool_loop.py:203-272,363-367`). Iteration budgets are DB `system_settings` policy, not code — chat is seeded at 25 and raises if unseeded (`orchestrator/core/seeds/seed_system_settings.py:566-570`). Voice and the storefront widget consume the identical `stream_response_with_agent` path (`orchestrator/api/chat_voice.py:132`; `orchestrator/api/widgets/chat.py:417`), so three surfaces share one loop by construction. The moat panel (M1) voted supports on code-liveness and supports on operator-value — the surfaces a paying merchant touches on day one are exactly the ones this loop powers. Be honest about the calibration: the prior-art lens killed the *moat* framing, because a client-owned tool loop is the vendor-documented reference pattern (Anthropic's docs, the Claude Agent SDK, OpenAI's Agents SDK). This is competent, table-stakes engineering executed correctly — a foundation, not a differentiator. The load-bearing caveat is F001 (CONFIRMED): a renamed kwarg at `orchestrator/modules/agents/factory/agent_factory.py:1070` severs every *non-chat* execution path, so this strength is real for chat, voice, and widget only.

**The board dispatch spine is live and correct, and it is genuinely OS-shaped.** This is the cleanest piece of systems engineering in the platform. Exactly-once claim is done with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` — deliberately safe to run in every uvicorn worker, unlike the fcntl-locked schedulers — with `pg_notify` wakeups, a lease sweeper, per-agent slots, SLA scan, Q44 reject-with-feedback and Run-Now (`orchestrator/services/board_dispatcher.py:92-268`). It is fronted by a deterministic pre-queue approval gate and a per-task dollar admission gate with block/defer/pause semantics (`orchestrator/core/services/approval_policy.py:138-182`; `orchestrator/modules/coordination/dispatcher.py:478-547`). The moat panel (M5) voted supports on code-liveness. This is the correct spine for an OS: it is where ambient triggers become admitted, governed work. Its value is currently blunted by two CONFIRMED caveats — leases are never renewed mid-run, so a run over ten minutes is swept and double-executed (F024), and the spine marks failed executions "done" with `result=None` because it never checks the returned error dict (F023, `orchestrator/api/board_tasks.py:900-921`). Both are small, localized diffs. The spine itself is sound.

**The retrieval and context layer is the best-integrated slice in the codebase.** `build_retrieval_filters` is a real, tested, fail-closed choke point with canonical team normalization on every documents-table path (21 tests plus a 7-test tenancy matrix; `orchestrator/modules/rag/retrieval_filters.py:78-120`), and one `RAGService.retrieve` serves chat tools, widgets, planners, and RAG-test with no parallel retrieval stacks (`orchestrator/modules/rag/service.py:223`). `ContextService` is genuinely *the* single prompt assembler across all ten execution modes, and a model-aware token budgeter is shared across retrieval, tool results, agent document reads, and the planning pack (`orchestrator/modules/rag/budget.py:56-72`). This is the OS thesis working: one grounding path, one budgeter, feeding the one loop. (The critical S3-backend tenant leak, F005, applies only when the non-default `S3_VECTORS_ENABLED` backend is on; it is in Section 3, and does not undercut the default choke point.)

**The API surface is the most mature layer, with disciplined narrow extensions instead of forks.** Roughly 104 mounted routers behind one shared hybrid-auth dependency that threads `workspace_id` everywhere; the PRD-143 observability inversion is real and tested (14 routers super_admin-locked at router level, fail-closed, with a dedicated 403 matrix); and the PRD-09 SDK-key board seam is exemplary least-privilege — empty permissions grant nothing, and the workspace is bound to the key and can never be overridden by a header (`orchestrator/core/auth/hybrid.py:527-539,594-606`). This is competent engineering that keeps the OS coherent as it grows.

**The graph-native capabilities are all real code in the live path — held honestly.** Verification voted 4/4 supports on code-liveness across the four graph assets (operating graph M2, commerce KG M3, code graph M4, field memory M6): semantic platform-action selection is default-on in chat via in-process cosine over per-action embeddings; the learned-edge write pipeline logs every execution and recomputes Wilson-bounded edges nightly, scheduled unconditionally at boot; the code graph is a 15-language tenancy-enforced read capability delivered to every agent; and field memory writes provenance-carrying digests into the dispatched agent's prompt. Everything claimed exists. But the calibration matters and the founder asked for truth: the panel voted prior-art *kills* on the commerce and code graphs and *weakens* on the routing and field claims, and operator-value *weakens* on all four. None of these mechanisms is novel (ToolNet, Zep, Cursor's index, Generative Agents all predate them), and today the flywheels write without reading — learned edges reach only the filtered strategy and offline eval, not default live routing (F015), Composio collapses to a single graph node (F016), and code webhooks are dead (F022). The one defensible lane the field-scan leaves open is per-tenant, outcome-labeled graph data compounding in production — a data moat, not a mechanism moat — and it closes with roughly a quarter of named, small diffs. Pitch it as a data-flywheel-in-progress or not at all.

**The Shopify/INBUILD pilot machinery has one genuinely deployed autonomous leg, and it is the right one.** The proactive widget path is live on inbuilduk.com: theme blocks feed the CDN loader, per-workspace plugin dispatch routes to the vertical (`orchestrator/api/widgets/chat.py:196-217`), and the opener/cart-idle path walks frequently-bought-with, collection, and vendor edges over the workspace graph to build a grounded directive fed into the real agent loop — byte-tested against a fixture snapshot (`orchestrator/integrations/shopify/widget_proactive.py:50-236`; `test_widget_proactive.py:349-417`). Auto reads the *same* workspace graph the widget uses (`orchestrator/modules/tools/discovery/handlers_graph.py:40-42`), the orders graph is privacy-by-design (Product-Product edges only, never customer or order nodes, `orchestrator/modules/knowledge/graph_extraction.py:685-688`), and PRD-141 vertical isolation is CI-enforced (`scripts/ci/check-no-shopify-in-generic.sh`). The architecturally uncommon piece — and the part worth defending — is that widget and Auto share one tenant graph behind a CI-enforced plugin seam that generalizes to a second vertical on the read path. The caveats are real and in Section 3: the graph is manual-refresh-only (webhooks no-op, F032), `/sync` is unauthenticated (F003), and the mappers that ingest the whole moat have zero tests with the end-to-end journey skipped (F091). But the deployed leg is the correct first proof.

**Where competent engineering is not moat, say so.** Field memory's shared scoring module is clean and honest (`orchestrator/modules/context/field_scoring.py:27-109` with real Qdrant health checks, no hardcoded `'healthy'`), and self-learning is substantially live across eight real outcome-to-signal loops — but the compounding claim is aspiration until the workspace field actually reaches planning and new missions, and the durable memory layer is itself a mem0 fork. These are strengths of execution, not defensibility. The moat, when it exists, is the accumulation — per-tenant operating graphs a competitor cannot bootstrap from zero — and today the write side of that flywheel is live while the read side is roadmap.

## 3. What to DUMP

Delete roughly six thousand lines of dead and rival code that survive only because nothing was retired, and the platform gets cheaper to maintain, less confusing to read, and — in three cases — genuinely more coherent as a single OS. This section is advisory: you decide. Every item below is verified to have no live caller on the path it pretends to serve, or to be a second implementation of something that already works. The pattern is unfinished migrations, not sloppiness — new engines landed, the old ones were never unmounted, and the codebase now offers multiple answers to "how does this work?", the exact ambiguity that degrades an agent reading its own substrate. Removal is safe where a finding confirms zero inbound references; two items carry a guard note where a partial reference or a build-time workaround means "excise the dead core, keep the one live thread."

### Rival execution and context stacks (the coherence wins)

Three deletions do more than save lines — they remove a competing answer from the OS spine.

The largest is the **legacy workflow engine**. `orchestrator/api/workflows.py` is 1,543 lines with three live execute endpoints (`orchestrator/api/workflows.py:1049`), mounted at `orchestrator/main.py:885-886` alongside `workflow_templates.py`, whose own comment reads "Legacy - backward compatibility" (F078). PRD-125, the document meant to retire it, is still `Status: Draft` and never landed. The result is that five execution engines coexist and "playbook" means three different things across the codebase — the single worst source of "which path actually runs?" confusion in the backend, sitting directly on the missions/board spine where Auto's autonomous work is supposed to converge. One caution: `modules/workflows/recipes/jira_bug_triage.py` is *not* zero-reference — it is registered under `workflow_id=9000` and reachable through the Composio webhook dispatch path (`orchestrator/api/composio.py:839-846`). So the retirement is a scoped migration (move that one recipe onto the mission path, then unmount the routers), not a blind `rm`. It collapses five engines toward one and is the highest-leverage coherence gain on this list.

Second, the **rival context stacks** in RAG. `orchestrator/api/context_policy.py` drives a `ContextAssembler` doing char-budget assembly (a direct rival to the live, model-aware token budgeter); `SearchService`/`ContextRetrievalEngine` (673 lines, docstring "main entry point for search") has zero callers outside its own package; and `ContextOptimizer` (950 lines of knapsack/MMR at `modules/search/optimization/context_optimizer.py`) is constructed at `modules/rag/service.py:185` but has zero method calls — the live path borrows only its `ContextItem` dataclass and budgets via PRD-157's `modules/rag/budget.select_within_budget` (F079). The architecture review is explicit: "delete the rival context stacks so one budgeter rules." Coherence needs exactly one prompt assembler and one budgeter; today a grep for "context budget" returns three, and an agent reasoning about its own context window could bind to the wrong one.

Third, the **dead NL2SQL `intelligence/` package** — 1,641 LOC across `SmartNL2SQLAgent`, `QueryClarifier`, `ResultExplainer`, a rephraser and a visualizer (`orchestrator/modules/nl2sql/__init__.py:61-68`). The live `smart_query` tool that names this package is a keyword router to analyze/query only and never touches it; a repo-wide grep for the class names returns only a docstring, one test, and a metadata tag. The legacy PRD-21 service methods (`service.py:680-883`) are equally dead — `_execute_query` even calls an undefined `_build_connection_string`, proving it cannot run (F080). This package is pure decoy: it advertises capability (clarification, explanation, visualization) the OS does not have, misleading both a reader and any planner inspecting the tool surface.

### Legacy channel and learning scaffolds

The **channels layer** carries a parallel legacy adapter set alongside the live driver system. The seven driverless adapters (teams, google_chat, signal, imessage, irc, matrix, line — exactly 1,589 lines) are reachable only via a manual `POST /api/channels/{id}/start` and one tool, and `_ping_platform_legacy` (`orchestrator/api/channels.py:143`) is fully dead with zero callers (F081). The verifier corrected two overclaims worth honoring: the four legacy adapters for *driver* platforms (telegram/slack/discord/whatsapp) are **not** dead — `ChannelManager.start_all`/`_create_adapter` (`orchestrator/channels/manager.py:139-151`) still instantiate them at boot for active polling rows — and the `workspace.settings.integrations` bag is load-bearing because it gates webhook replies. So the safe dump is narrow: delete `_ping_platform_legacy` outright, and treat the seven driverless adapters as removable only once you accept dropping those advertised-but-unimplemented platforms. The coherence value is honesty: the platform claims twelve channels but drives five, and the dead adapters make the count look real.

The **learning decoys** are the clearest "delete for clarity" case. `modules/learning`'s `patterns/` and `feedback/` are 0-byte packages and its `PlaybookMiner` mines hardcoded demo sequences; `modules/evaluation` is a TODO scaffold with `__all__=[]` and zero importers (F082). The real self-optimization loops live entirely elsewhere — `services/harness_service.py`, `core/services/edge_builder.py`, `modules/tools/discovery`. Two caveats: `modules/learning` is imported by the dead-on-arrival `api_playbooks` surface (F069), so it is "consumer-free" only in that its one consumer is itself dead; and the nightly `failed_after` graph edges are write-only bookkeeping by design (the negative signal reaches scoring via a separate `fails_for_intent` affinity), noise rather than a broken loop. Deleting the two scaffold packages removes the most misleading signpost in the repo: a directory literally named `learning` that contains none of the learning.

### Frontend relics and misfiled API modules

The **API mount-honesty cluster** is small but embarrassing: `api/workspace_exec.py` is a real router (`orchestrator/api/workspace_exec.py:20`) never imported anywhere, while a live duplicate serves the same `/exec` path from `api/workspace_files.py:160`; `api/anthropic_client.py` is an LLM-provider class misfiled under `api/` that imports a nonexistent `api/base` (`:12`) and would `ImportError` if touched; and `analytics.py` is mounted at a bare `/analytics` with no `/api` prefix (F083). The verifier refuted the `sites.py` leg — it is mounted and has a live Settings-page caller — so leave `sites.py` alone and delete the other three. Each is a landmine for a code-reading agent that assumes files under `api/` are routers and imports resolve.

Finally, the **dead-but-routed frontend relics** (F084): `/api-control` (sole importer of a `use-api-toggle` hook whose localStorage key nothing reads), an empty Prisma vertical (`schema.prisma` with 0 models, `lib/db.ts` with 0 importers), `usePageAPI` (17 pages calling an explicit no-op `setCurrentPage`), a 442-line `/styleguide` live in prod, and three coexisting lockfiles (`package-lock.json`, `pnpm-lock.yaml`, `yarn.lock`) that make builds nondeterministic. The lockfile cleanup alone is worth doing this week — pick one package manager. Guard: `pages/_document.tsx` is a deliberate workaround against a `/_document` build failure, so verify the build before deleting that one item.

## 4. What to FIX

Fix the async execution engine first: F001 alone makes the whole autonomous half of the OS a silent no-op while every dashboard reads healthy. The pattern across the register is the one the prior review named — the components are real and the last mile is severed — so the fixes below are ordered by how directly each restores Auto's ability to act as conductor rather than leaving nineteen instruments playing alone. Every finding here carries a CONFIRMED or ADJUSTED verdict against pinned commit `37fdecc4e`; nothing in this section is speculative.

### Critical — fix before anything ships autonomously

**F001 — the non-chat agent engine is dead on every surface.** `orchestrator/modules/agents/factory/agent_factory.py:1070` passes `content_truncate_chars` to a `ToolLoopExecutor` constructor that PRD-157 renamed to accept only `content_truncate_tokens` (`tool_loop.py:154`), with no shim. Every board task, mission, scheduled run, webhook reply and inter-agent delegation therefore raises a `TypeError` after its first LLM response, the retry `except` swallows it, budget is burned, and the call returns `{"status":"error"}`; PRD-161's exactly-once dispatch spine faithfully delivers every task into this dead engine. Chat, voice and widget build their own executor correctly and are unaffected — which is exactly why it hid. Fix: rename the kwarg to `content_truncate_tokens=0` and add a test that builds the executor *through* `execute_with_prompt` rather than mocking the factory. A one-line change gating the entire "Jarvis acting out" thesis; it comes before every other item.

**F002, F003, F006 — unauthenticated or unscoped tenant surfaces that let one caller reach across workspaces.** Three routers are mounted in production with no workspace scoping. The skills router (`orchestrator/api/skills.py:838,731,604`) takes `ctx` and never uses it: any workspace can attach another's private SKILL.md to its agent (prompt-injection and exfiltration), read any skill's content, and a single `DELETE /{skill_id}` can deactivate a global builtin-core skill and lobotomise every workspace's Auto (F002). The four Shopify sync routes (`orchestrator/api/shopify.py:584-991`) declare only `db=Depends(get_db)`, so guessing a workspace UUID triggers costly Composio bulk-ops and overwrites that workspace's knowledge graph via `import_graph(merge=False)` (F003). The legacy workflow execute endpoints (`orchestrator/api/workflows.py:1059-1072`) fetch the workflow with no workspace filter and select the first active agent from *any* workspace; under the default `local` runner this is a cross-tenant existence oracle, under `queued` it enqueues a real cross-workspace `AgentTask` (F006, ADJUSTED). Fix: add a workspace-scoped auth dependency to the four sync routes; filter Agent and Skill by `ctx.workspace_id` and require ownership or super-admin for global-skill delete; scope the workflow/agent queries to `ctx.workspace_id`, or delete the PRD-125 router (preferred — its engine is already a disabled stub).

**F004, F007 — fail-open auth defaults.** The orchestrator's `/api/shopify/*` provisioning surface is fail-open when `SHOPIFY_INTERNAL_API_KEY` is unset: `config.py:441` defaults it to `""` and `_verify_internal_key` (`shopify.py:43-45`) returns early on a falsy key, so any request carrying *any* `Authorization` header value is accepted (F004, ADJUSTED — the header is required, so a missing header 422s, but `Authorization: Bearer x` sails through the fail-open branch to `/provision`, `/connect` and `/deactivate`). Separately, `GET /api/alerts` is unconditionally unauthenticated; the `ALERT_INGEST_TOKEN` bearer check guards only the ingest POST, so setting the token does not close the alerts read surface (F007, ADJUSTED). Fix: require the Shopify key fail-closed at boot and set it in Railway; wrap both monitoring routers in `require_super_admin` (they are the obs tier PRD-143 defines) and keep the bearer token only for AlertManager ingest.

**F005 — the RAG choke point applies no workspace filter on the default path.** `S3VectorsBackend.search()` accepts a `filters` param and never applies it (`s3_vectors_backend.py:123-146`); tenant isolation rests entirely on a bucket-name template containing `{workspace_id}`, and nothing validates that `S3_VECTORS_BUCKET` embeds the placeholder. On the default no-team path the choke point applies no post-filter (`rag/service.py:316-317`) and `_expand_to_parent_context` hydrates chunks with no workspace scoping, so a shared bucket leaks cross-workspace chunk text straight into LLM context (F005, ADJUSTED; gated by `S3_VECTORS_ENABLED=true`). Fix: fail startup if the resolved bucket name lacks `workspace_id`, and pass a `workspace_id` metadata filter that drops non-matching hits.

**F008, F009, F010 — the open-core / one-core-two-deployments thesis is not deployable from zero.** Three distinct root causes, all triple-voted. The frontend mounts `ClerkProvider` unconditionally (`frontend/components/providers.tsx:33`) with `clerkMiddleware auth.protect()` on all non-public routes and zero edition flag anywhere, so without a Clerk tenant the UI serves nothing and PRD-150's frontend half never landed (F008). A fresh clone cannot boot: `docker-compose.yml:35` mounts the initdb schema from a path that does not exist (the real file is `orchestrator/core/database/init_complete_schema.sql`), so a fresh volume gets an empty DB and the frontend never starts (F009). ## 5. What is MISSING

The gap between what Automatos has and what the OS vision requires is not a gap in components — it is a gap in circuits and in one governing plane. Nineteen domains are individually live; what is missing is the connective tissue that would make Auto a conductor rather than nineteen instruments in one room. The absences below are grouped by what they block: OS coherence, enterprise grade (per the bar in Section 13, not repeated here), and the first vertical. Every claim is a CONFIRMED or ADJUSTED finding.

### Missing for OS coherence

**The policy plane does not exist as a plane.** Budgets, approvals, rate-limits and roles live in three partial mechanisms — per-router auth dependencies, action-registry flags, and the mission approval engine — with no single configuration evaluated in one place; guardrails stop at the mission boundary and the API surface has no awareness of `approval_policy` (F085, `orchestrator/core/services/approval_policy.py`, mission-runtime only). This directly contradicts the "guardrails as policy, not code" principle: the enforcement point that would let Auto act autonomously *within bounds* is absent, so the choice today is babysit-everything or trust-blindly.

**There is no pre-call budget admission gate on the hot path.** No budget-exceeded exception class exists in the repo; the tool loop, chat, agent-factory and recipe loops all issue LLM and tool calls with no spend check, and the LLM manager only cost-logs *after* the call (F086, `orchestrator/modules/tools/execution/tool_loop.py:326`). The one admission gate sits at mission-task dispatch and reads a per-run ceiling, not the workspace plan — `Workspace.plan_limits` is consulted only for concurrency caps in `orchestrator/services/concurrency_guard.py:69`, never for cost or tokens. "Composing capabilities under a budget" is unbacked at the level where spend happens.

**The learning loop is write-mostly, so the operating graph never compounds from live traffic.** The mechanism is wired — telemetry to nightly edge recompute to `GraphRouter` — but learned `used_after` edges and affinities reach only the FILTERED loading strategy and offline eval, not default chat routing, and the prompt-catalog chain hints are gated behind `TOOL_ROUTING_GRAPH` (default false, `orchestrator/config.py:680`) (F015, ADJUSTED). Two return paths compound this: `rag_feedback` is a write-only signal that feeds nothing back into ranking or any eval (F070, `orchestrator/api/rag_feedback.py:50-70`), and the memory gradient has **no field-to-durable promotion seam** — patterns decay and are hard-deleted before becoming durable memory (per the memory-field digest; the compaction sweep in `orchestrator/modules/context/adapters/vector_field.py` deletes without promoting). The circuits meant to make the system "smarter over time" are open at their return path.

**Real-time push to the operating surfaces does not exist.** The Command Centre lede claims "Streaming live," but every ops surface polls on 8–60s intervals, the board SSE endpoint has zero frontend subscribers, and the SSE is itself only a timed ping, not a `LISTEN/NOTIFY` stream (F090, `frontend/components/command-center/command-center-shell.tsx:110`, `orchestrator/api/board_tasks.py:417`). Auto can act, but the human cannot watch it act in real time — the "Jarvis acting out" half of the loop is decorative.

Two disconnects sever advertised seams: per-channel agent pinning is written and advertised but no routing path ever reads `channel_connections.default_agent_id` (F029, `orchestrator/core/models/channels.py:38`), and governance covers missions only — board tasks and playbook runs Auto creates have no ceiling or approval gate, while five execution engines coexist and two (legacy workflow runner, `/api/tasks` direct-step) sit outside all governance yet are mounted (F060, `orchestrator/api/tasks.py:33`, `orchestrator/main.py:885`).

### Missing for enterprise (multi-tenant SaaS grade)

Against the Section 13 bar, four absences are *structurally* missing rather than merely buggy. First, **there is no unified policy plane** (F085, above): the Governance pillar has no single enforcement point, so audit-log completeness and staged compliance have no substrate to attach to. Second, **there is no backup or disaster recovery anywhere in the repo** — no `pg_dump` tooling, no volume-snapshot scripts, no restore runbook for the main pgvector database or the separate mem0 instance; the only script present is a DDL developer tool (F050, `scripts/`). Local runs on unmanaged volumes and SaaS relies implicitly on Railway snapshots, failing the Reliability bar outright.

Third, the **CI and supply-chain lanes an enterprise bar requires are absent**: no coverage measurement against the 80% doctrine, no dependency audit or SAST (CodeQL unconfigured, no `dependabot.yml`), and — most consequentially — no migration-replay lane, because CI initialises schema via `create_all` and never runs Alembic, which is why the four-heads state on main shipped undetected (F092, ADJUSTED, `.github/workflows/test.yml:99-100`; GitHub-native secret scanning is enabled, a real mitigation). Fourth, the **frontend-to-backend route-contract net PRD-168's deletion wave relied on was never built** — PRD-155 S2 landed with zero frontend files and nothing consumes `route-manifest.json` from the frontend, so "CI fails when the frontend calls a non-existent path" is untrue (F044, `orchestrator/scripts`). Remaining enterprise gaps — unauthenticated tenant endpoints, plaintext tokens, RBAC inversions — sit in the defects section against Section 13's tenancy and security pillars.

### Missing for the first vertical (Shopify)

The vertical is missing the pieces that would let Auto *operate* the merchant, not just chat about it. **There is no local object storage** (PRD-151's MinIO / `S3_ENDPOINT_URL`), so the knowledge flywheel fail-softs to `None` on every output and the OSS edition never learns from its own work, while generated documents live only on ephemeral disk (F089, `orchestrator/services/knowledge_flywheel.py:216-223`). **The commerce knowledge graph — the vertical's entire moat — is unverified at its ingestion boundary**: the two catalog/orders mappers have zero direct tests and the two headline commerce-KG journeys are skipped, so a mapper regression silently corrupts every downstream opener and cross-sell (F091, `orchestrator/modules/knowledge/graph_extraction.py:503,693`, `test_golden_journeys.py:101,221`). The vertical also lacks an autonomous inbound-email path and a generic vertical-provision abstraction, so vertical #2 would fork `api/shopify.py` wholesale (detailed in Section 10). The through-line: the synchronous chat plane works, but every asynchronous leg the flagship demo needs has either no executor to run in or no verified data to run on.

## 6. Blind spots - what you are not considering

The most dangerous risks here are not in the verified code; they are in the assumptions the roadmap is silent on. Written from a deliberately context-starved position - what a hostile diligence partner raises that the plan does not test - the twelve items are ranked by expected damage over a twelve-to-eighteen-month horizon, each with why it bites, the earliest tripwire, and the cheapest hedge. Verified findings are cited where a blind spot touches one. Competitive mechanics are argued in Sections 8 and 11; here the concern is only what you are not planning for.

**1. Shopify is your best-funded competitor, not your proving ground - and it has already shipped.** It has re-architected around a bundled, effectively free agentic assistant embedded in the merchant admin, while your equivalent is a paid add-on; the platform absorbs the eighty percent of "ask Auto to run my store" use cases first, leaving you the long tail plus a channel Shopify taxes and governs. Tripwire: your pilot says "the built-in assistant already does that," or its next release adds scheduled agent missions. Hedge: keep a living list of the ten cross-system missions the incumbent structurally cannot do - work spanning Shopify plus email, ads, logistics and finance with tenancy and audit - re-validate it each release, and keep the Shopify layer thin so the wedge can rotate. Do not out-build the incumbent's assistant on its own turf.

**2. Autonomy unit economics: your best users become your worst customers.** The mission budget ceiling (PRD-163) is cost control, not margin, and is denominated on a flat rate the review confirmed is wrong by up to sixty times against the platform's own price map (F059, `coordinator_service.py:2384`); a genuinely ambient tenant plausibly costs more per month than a typical commerce-app subscription, so success inverts margin and you learn it only after pricing is anchored. Tripwire: the first tenant-month where spend exceeds the fee, or inability to answer "what did this tenant cost last week" in one query. Hedge: meter per-mission cost now on an honest price source, stand up a gross-margin view, price missions or outcomes rather than seats, and make the ambient layer webhook-driven, cheapest-model-triaged and prefix-cached on the tool catalog.

**3. Budgets cap dollars; nothing you have built caps reach.** An agent with roughly two hundred tools and write access to live stores, email and payments across many tenants fails in correlated, irreversible ways - one bad playbook or injected instruction can execute fleet-wide - and the review confirmed the substrate is absent: the main-plane rate limiter throttles nothing (F040, `main.py:762-776`), there is no pre-call admission gate or budget-exceeded exception (F086), and governance covers missions only, leaving board tasks, playbooks, the recipe executor and the direct-step engine ungoverned (F060, `dispatcher.py:452-457`). One incident is every customer's trust event, since all tenants share one codebase. Tripwire: the first "undo that" you cannot honour, or a mission executing dozens of writes with no human in the loop. Hedge: a write-op ledger with a compensating-action inventory for the top twenty destructive tools, per-tool rate caps inside the Wave-4 policy plane, canary-tenant playbook rollout, and a rehearsed kill switch - extending your own "guardrails as policy" thesis from budget to blast radius.

**4. Shared field memory is a persistent-injection amplifier and a deletion surface.** One prompt-injected trajectory can write into workspace-shared memory that then steers every agent across sessions - memory poisoning is a named top agentic threat, and the review found compaction hard-deletes with no promotion path and a running mission shadows workspace recall (F021), so the promotion arm you would build is the unguarded surface. Separately, Shopify data is customer personal information and UK right-to-erasure must cascade through vector stores, snapshots, promoted memories and learned edges - derived data nobody architects for deletion - and this binds now via the UK pilot. One "Auto mentioned another customer's order" or one unanswerable erasure request ends the pilot in a week. Tripwire: the first end-customer erasure request, or any retrieval surfacing content the asking user could not reach through the manual UI. Hedge: tag every memory item with provenance and data-subject identity at write time, classify personal information on write, ship erasure-by-subject now, and never promote a trajectory with untrusted external content without a taint flag - your "ask-Auto equals manual-UI" parity goal doubles as the access-control test.

**5. The operating-graph moat has a vendor-bundling half-life measured in quarters.** Semantic tool selection over a large catalog is already a first-party vendor feature and the learned-edge idea has direct prior art (Section 8); what a vendor cannot bundle is your tenants' data and audit harness, but the review found the loop that accumulates it is not closed - the tool intermediary collapses to a single graph node so the eight-hundred-app surface never learns (F016, `telemetry.py:56-64`) and the metadata pipeline is orphaned and fails open (F018). You keep investing in routing the vendor gives away, and the moat slide stops surviving diligence. Tripwire: a quarterly benchmark of graph-informed routing against vanilla vendor tool-search shows single-digit lift, or a lab ships persistent tool-preference memory. Hedge: build that benchmark - it does not exist - reframe the moat as proprietary per-tenant data plus the policy plane, and architect to swap the vendor primitive in underneath your graph.

**6. Merged is not deployed, deployed is not adopted - the cadence has no soak stage.** Sixteen PRDs merged in three weeks then eighteen days of silence; the open-core north star (PRDs 150 to 153) has zero trace on main; code-canvas (PRD-170) exists only as an unpushed checkpoint labelled "not for main"; and main carries four alembic heads making `alembic upgrade head` ambiguous and deploys a manual exercise (F008, F009, F010, F068). Measuring "PR merged" accumulates code nobody has run and comprehension debt, since no human wrote most of it, so the pilot hits a defect on a fragile deploy path and "one core, two deployments" is silently untrue. Tripwire: the next deploy requires hand-resolving migration heads, or a pilot bug in a recently-merged feature with no telemetry showing it ran. Hedge: one hardening week - collapse the alembic heads, deploy head-of-main to the pilot, redefine "done" as "used with telemetry" - then explicitly decide whether open-core is on or off the strategy.

**7. The tool intermediary is a single point of platform failure inside your compliance story.** The entire tool surface routes through one Series-A intermediary, and the coupling reaches the trust boundary - the review found forged agentic webhook events logged and then allowed through on an unauthenticated endpoint "for debugging" (F028, `composio.py:630-632`). Unpriced risks: repricing at your scale inflection (their price is your cost floor), downtime as a platform-wide outage, acquisition or terms pivot in a layer labs are shopping, and OAuth-app ownership - if customer connections live on their OAuth apps, migration means every tenant re-authorises. They are also a subprocessor seeing tokens and payloads, which lands in your data-processing agreement and audit scope. Tripwire: a pricing or terms diff, rising error rates, or a pilot security review asking "who is this third party?" Hedge: own the OAuth apps natively for the top three integrations - Shopify above all - keep the execution interface abstract with a first-party protocol fallback, and negotiate price-lock and data-egress terms while small.

**8. The solo-founder enterprise paradox: the bar you chose requires an organisation you do not have.** SOC2 Type II needs months of operating evidence so the clock starts now, and change-approval and separation-of-duties are structurally awkward when one human reviews a fleet that merged sixteen PRDs in three weeks; the review confirms the substrate gap - no coverage measurement, dependency audit, SAST, or migration-replay lane, which is why the four-heads state shipped undetected (F092, `.github/workflows/test.yml`). A bus factor of one is a disqualifier no architecture answers. Tripwire: the first security questionnaire or "who is on call?" from procurement, or an auditor asking who approves the founder's own merges. Hedge: start evidence-automation tooling now, document runbooks and code escrow as the bus-factor answer, sell the pilot as an honest design-partner arrangement not on enterprise SLAs, and configure branch protection so the harness is the second reviewer of record.

**9. Self-learning without a frozen evaluation is self-poisoning you cannot see.** Learned edges trained on the agent's own trajectories inherit early routing bias, "success" labels are noisy, and an injected instruction can be learned - turning one-shot injection into a persistent routing backdoor; there is an unchosen fork - cross-tenant learning contaminates while per-tenant learning cold-starts and weakens the moat claim - and the review found edge reads have no workspace filter (F016 context), so the design leans toward contamination by omission. Tripwire: you cannot answer "what is routing accuracy this month versus last," or a strange tool choice recurs across sessions. Hedge: freeze a golden intent-to-tool evaluation set before enabling edge updates in production, gate updates behind accuracy thresholds with decay, and provenance-tag every learned edge to its teaching trajectory so poisoning is auditable and revocable.

**10. Liability lands before the AI Act does, and in a different country than your roadmap.** The Act's high-risk obligations are deferred and an autonomous merchant agent likely is not high-risk anyway; what bites sooner is product-liability and national fault law, and case law already holds a company answers for its agent's statements - when Auto misprices an item or promises a refund, the merchant absorbs it and your contract decides who pays, while your paying customer is UK (UK data-protection law, no AI Act) and the roadmap aims at the EU. Tripwire: the first customer-facing autonomous action your policy permits without approval, or an insurer quote excluding autonomous agents. Hedge: point the approval-policy engine at liability categories - default customer-facing comms and financial writes to "ask" - keep the action ledger as evidence, get an insurance quote now to learn insurer requirements, disclose AI in customer-facing output by default, and sequence compliance UK-first.

**11. One pilot, no demand engine, and the self-host window closing.** A single paying pilot overfits the roadmap and zeroes revenue and reference on churn; the stated moat is entirely supply-side with no community or marketplace flywheel, which matters because the open-source competitors' moat is community velocity (Section 11), and the positioning gift - "the agent OS you are allowed to run at work," against rivals with serious unpatched-instance security problems - is only real if self-host ships, yet PRDs 150 to 153 have zero trace on main. The window is open now and the feature has been designed twice, shipped zero times - the most expensive state. Tripwire: an open-source rival announcing teams and role-based access with hosting, or pilot feature requests consuming over half the PRD queue. Hedge: recruit two or three unpaid design partners in different Shopify segments, and make a one-page kill-or-commit decision on open-core this month.

## 7. Capability-composability audit

The platform is a genuinely composable fabric for reads and interactive chat, and a set of feature silos for writes and autonomy. Verified against `automatos-ai` main at `37fdecc4e`, the substrate is real: 161 registered platform actions, 32 promoted to first-class tool schemas and 7 confined to super_admin, all delivered through one source of truth — `get_tools_for_agent` in `orchestrator/modules/tools/tool_router.py:220` (docstring marked "SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH" at :230) — wired into chat at `consumers/chatbot/service.py:694`. A single chat agent reaches RAG, NL2SQL, both graphs, durable and field memory, missions, playbooks, deliverables and the Composio catalogue in one tool loop. That is the Jarvis substrate, and it works — but only while a human watches the chat window, because one wrong keyword argument amputates every headless surface from the same fabric, and the guardrail plane covers only one of three tool lanes.

### The one fact that reframes every "yes" below

Every "agent-invocable today" entry in the table is chat-true but autonomy-false. All non-chat surfaces — board dispatch, missions, scheduled tasks, heartbeats, channel webhooks, inter-agent delegation, and the PRD-161 local task runner — funnel through `AgentFactory.execute_with_prompt`, which builds its tool loop by passing `content_truncate_chars=0` at `orchestrator/modules/agents/factory/agent_factory.py:1070` to a keyword-only `ToolLoopExecutor.__init__` that accepts only `content_truncate_tokens` (`tool_loop.py:148-156`). PRD-157 (`25f0789bd`) renamed the parameter and left no shim; a live reconstruction of the origin/main source raises `TypeError`. The call sits inside the retry block after the first LLM response (`agent_factory.py:1029`), so each attempt burns a real LLM call, the bare `except Exception` at :1201 swallows the crash, and the caller receives `{"status": "error"}`. The blast radius covers every autonomous entry point (`board_tasks.py:893`, `webhooks.py:636`, `scheduled_task_service.py:401`, `heartbeat_service.py:924`, `inter_agent.py:719/792`, the mission dispatcher, and `core/task_runner/local.py`); chat alone escapes, because it constructs the loop directly with the correct kwarg (`service.py:1457`). PRD-161's exactly-once dispatch spine therefore delivers every task faithfully into a dead engine. Until this line is fixed, "compose everything" means "compose everything by hand, in chat."

### Governance-plane parity gaps beneath the table

Two verified parity findings fork behaviour across planes rather than gate one capability. SDK-key permission semantics fork on the same key record: an empty or null permissions list means unrestricted on the widget plane and in `ApiKeyService.check_permissions` (`api/widgets/auth.py:239`, `core/services/api_key_service.py:237-241`) but deny-all on the board plane (`hybrid.py:537-539`), so one minted no-permission key is a god-key on one plane and a null-key on the other (F042). And the role hierarchy is inconsistent: seven admin routers gate with a strict `system_role == 'admin'` that 403s super_admin out of admin workspaces, plugins, marketplace and skills management, while `hybrid.py:694` and at least nine other sites treat super_admin as a superset of admin (F043). Both undercut the premise that governance is a coherent layer sitting cleanly beneath the tool fabric.

### Cross-cutting composition observations

The guardrail plane is policy on only one lane. Budget admission exists solely as a per-task check in the mission dispatcher (`modules/coordination/dispatcher.py:478-500`) that the mission creator can disable via `budget_pause_disabled` config; rate limits, confirmation and super_admin filtering bind `platform_*` actions only; and the Composio lane — the one that touches external money and messages — routes straight through `unified_executor.py:352-364` with none of it. The policy engine exists, but its coverage is an accident of tool prefix rather than a designed admission boundary. Relatedly, the admin gate is a no-op for agent-context calls: with `caller_context=None` it passes whenever the workspace has any owner or admin member, and `is_admin` auto-flips true for any workspace that merely has an owner (`platform_executor.py:641-645`, `tool_router.py:368-388`, F014).

The graph-native learning moat learns from `platform_*` tools only. Composio telemetry collapses every external action to a single `composio_execute` node, logging `action_name = tool_name[:255]` and only parameter keys, so `SLACK_SEND_MESSAGE` and its 855 siblings are indistinguishable in the learned graph and can never accrue `used_after` or affinity signal (`modules/tools/execution/telemetry.py:56-64`, F016). The operating graph therefore cannot learn cross-app sequencing — precisely the behaviour that would differentiate an orchestrator from a tool menu.

The recurring silo shape is the mutating verb that stayed on an HTTP router and never became a tool: codegraph index, commerce sync, document pin, channel send, template and brand authoring, board run-now and reject, git skill import. Reads compose almost everywhere; writes do not. Three cases invert — document generation, Composio actions and memory management are ask-Auto-only because no UI performs them — but these exceptions prove the rule that write-side composition was never finished.

### The five highest-leverage unlocks

1. **Fix the kwarg.** Change `content_truncate_chars` to `content_truncate_tokens` at `agent_factory.py:1070`. One line reactivates seven autonomous surfaces — board, missions, scheduled, heartbeat, webhooks, inter-agent, task-runner. Nothing else on this list matters until it lands.
2. **Move budget and approval admission into `unified_executor.execute()`** as a universal pre-tool-call gate covering the platform, Composio and workspace lanes, replacing the mission-dispatch-only, creator-disablable check. This is the single change that makes "guardrails are policy" actually true.
3. **Close the write-verb silos as a batch of thin tools** — `platform_send_channel_message` (wrapping the already-built `channels/sender.py`), codegraph index/reindex, board run-now/reject, commerce product-sync start/status, and declaring `template_id` in the `generate_document` schema (the runtime already handles it). Each is a wrapper over existing service code; together they collapse most of the parity column.
4. **Wire `jobs/sync_composio_actions.py` into the scheduler and add action-level Composio telemetry** so the metadata populates and the operating graph can learn cross-app `used_after` edges instead of a single collapsed node.
5. **Run a mode and channel parity pass** — route channel inbound through the same complexity path as chat (delete the keyword gate at `webhooks.py:392-437`), add memory to HEARTBEAT mode, give plan-mode chat a read-only tool subset instead of `use_tools = None`, and bind the field tools to the calling mission rather than an arbitrary running one.

### Verdict: fabric with gaps, not silos in a fabric costume

One composable fabric for reads and chat; feature silos for writes and autonomy. The architecture is genuinely fabric-shaped — a single registry, a single router, a single executor, and policy objects positioned in the right places — but today it behaves as a fabric only inside the interactive chat loop. One `TypeError` amputates every autonomous surface, the guardrail plane covers one of three tool lanes, roughly ten mutating verbs never left their HTTP routers, and the learning graph is blind to the external-tool catalogue. The decisive point in the platform's favour is that the fix profile is cheap: a kwarg, a gate relocation, a batch of thin wrappers over services that already exist, and a scheduler wiring. That cheapness is the strongest evidence that this is a fabric with gaps rather than silos in a fabric costume — the connective tissue is designed and present, and the remaining work is to thread the write-side and autonomous paths through it so that asking Auto and doing it by hand converge on the same capability.

## 8. Graph-native moat audit

The graph-native story is honest, verified plumbing plus exactly one open moat lane, and it survives investor scrutiny only if told as a data-flywheel-in-progress rather than as novel algorithms. All four assets the platform pitches as differentiation — the operating-graph tool router, the commerce knowledge graph, the code graph, and the shared field memory — are real code in the live path on `automatos-ai` main at `37fdecc4e`, yet none of the underlying mechanisms is novel; each has named, published, or first-party prior art a competitor can buy or clone. The only genuinely open lane the field-scan found is per-tenant, outcome-labeled graph data compounding in production, and in every asset the loop that would accumulate and consume that data is the part not yet closed. The net verdict is one genuine moat *candidate*, two pieces of competent-but-commoditized engineering, and one compounding claim still at aspiration.

A three-lens panel scored each asset on code-liveness, prior-art (is the mechanism novel), and operator-value (does a paying tenant feel it today). The pattern is the finding: everything *supports* on liveness, mechanisms are killed or weakened on novelty, and every asset is *weakened* on operator-value.

| Asset (claim) | Code-liveness | Prior-art (novelty) | Operator-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating-graph tool router (M2) | supports | weakens | weakens |
| Commerce knowledge graph (M3) | supports | kills | weakens |
| Code graph (M4) | supports | kills | weakens |
| Shared field memory (M6) | supports | weakens | weakens |

Every operator-value *weaken* shares one cause — the graphs are invisible to the tenant — which is the through-line here and the one cross-cutting fix: a "your workspace's graphs" report (edges learned, actions routed better, revenue assisted, memory retained) turns plumbing into felt, renewal-driving value and is what would join these four assets to the whole OS with Auto as orchestrator, since today they are substrate Auto reads from but a tenant never sees.

### Operating-graph tool router — competent engineering, and the only genuine moat candidate

This router is the most defensible of the four because the field-scan found no commercial platform visibly shipping a persistent, per-tenant, outcome-labeled tool graph driving production routing — but the data that would fill it is not yet captured, so the platform is not yet standing in the lane it could own. Semantic action selection is default-on and live: `SEMANTIC_TOOL_ROUTING` defaults true (`orchestrator/config.py:687-688`), narrowing the `platform_execute` enum by in-process cosine over per-action qwen3-embedding-8b vectors, Redis-cached (`orchestrator/modules/tools/tool_router.py:404-424`; `orchestrator/modules/tools/discovery/action_semantic_index.py:96-117`). The learned-edge write pipeline is end-to-end and restart-safe: every execution logs to `tool_execution_logs` in a finally-block (`orchestrator/modules/tools/execution/unified_executor.py:434-445`), and a nightly job recomputes `used_after`/`failed_after` edges and intent/agent affinities with Wilson lower bounds (`orchestrator/core/services/edge_builder.py:56,353,382`), registered unconditionally at boot so the graph populates regardless of any flag (`orchestrator/main.py:388-392`).

One correction, in the platform's favor: the learned edges **do** shape the default live tool list. The chat context mode declares the FILTERED loading strategy — the primary chat path — and its `GraphRouter.rank_chains` unconditionally folds learned `ToolRoutingEdge`/`ToolRoutingAffinity` rows into chain scores under the default-true semantic flag (F015, adjusted; `orchestrator/modules/tools/discovery/graph_router.py:150-171`). What learned trajectories do *not* reach by default is narrow — the prompt-catalog chain hints gated behind `TOOL_ROUTING_GRAPH` (default false, `orchestrator/config.py:693`) and the enum's pure-semantic narrowing — and the live effect is largely confined to core, pinned, and promoted tools: real reach, but shallow.

The moat problem is not a dead read side; it is that the data being read is neither per-tenant nor complete. Edge reads carry **no workspace filter** — zero `workspace_id` references in `graph_router.py` against per-workspace writes at `edge_builder.py:207-212` — which is both a tenancy defect and a direct moat-defeater, since an unpartitioned graph cannot become a tenant's private, un-bootstrappable asset. Two gaps starve it further: chat threads only `{user_id}` into caller context so intent edges never materialize (`orchestrator/consumers/chatbot/service.py:1317`), and the 856-app Composio surface — the merchant's real external tools — collapses to one graph node because telemetry logs `action_name=tool_name` (F016, confirmed; `orchestrator/modules/tools/execution/telemetry.py:58`).

The mechanism is commoditized: semantic top-N selection (tool-RAG) is first-party in Anthropic's Tool Search Tool (~85% context reduction; Opus 4.5 79.5%→88.1% on MCP-eval), with open equivalents in RAG-MCP (arXiv:2505.03275) and langgraph-bigtool; the learned-edge half has near-exact prior art in ToolNet (arXiv:2403.00839) — a directed tool graph with trajectory edges (`used_after`) re-weighted by call-success scoring (`succeeds_for_intent`) — and the Wilson bound is a stock statistic. Defensibility within one to two quarters is small and inverts the gaps above — `workspace_id`-scope the edge reads, thread the query into caller context, log the resolved Composio action name — then publish a monthly per-tenant eval of edge-informed versus embedding-only accuracy. The switching cost then becomes the tenant's own operational history, and this asset joins Auto as orchestrator most directly, since its data is Auto's own action stream across every channel.

### Commerce knowledge graph — competent engineering, novelty killed

The commerce graph is real end-to-end and shared correctly between the widget and Auto, but its novelty is dead and its compounding value is forfeited by its own privacy design, so it reads as good vertical engineering, not a moat. Shopify bulk operations feed `map_shopify_catalog`/`map_shopify_orders` through `GraphifyService.import_graph` into Postgres `workspace_graphs` (`orchestrator/api/shopify.py:675-679,891-933`; `orchestrator/core/graph_storage.py:62,99`), creating only aggregated Product-to-Product co-occurrence edges, never customer or order nodes (`orchestrator/modules/knowledge/graph_extraction.py:685-688`). The proactive cart-idle path walks frequently-bought-together, collection, and vendor edges into a grounded opener fed to the real agent loop (`orchestrator/integrations/shopify/widget_proactive.py:60,101,123`), and Auto reads the same workspace graph (`handlers_graph.py:40-42,123`) behind a CI-enforced PRD-141 isolation seam — the one architecturally uncommon piece.

The weaknesses keep it off the moat list. The graph is a manual-refresh snapshot: catalog and order webhooks are a silent no-op against it (F032, adjusted; `orchestrator/api/shopify.py:491-518`), and the `/sync` endpoints are unauthenticated (F003, confirmed critical). The two headline mappers have zero tests and the end-to-end journey is a skipped test (F091, confirmed). There is no measurement surface anywhere in `api/widgets/` — no opener-impression, fallback, or assisted-revenue tracking — so at day 30 a merchant has an anecdote, not a number. On novelty, frequently-bought-together cross-sell is Amazon's 2003 item-to-item collaborative filtering, commoditized on Shopify by Rebuy, LimeSpot, and Shopify's own free Search & Discovery, while Shopify platformizes catalog access itself via Catalog MCP and UCP-with-Google — giving away the leg the graph is built on. The defensible version within a quarter is the fusion Shopify will not do — consented per-tenant edges joining orders, widget conversations, executed campaign actions, and outcomes, surfaced as an "assisted revenue this month" number — needing only webhook-driven freshness, authenticated sync, and that measurement surface.

### Code graph — competent engineering, not a moat

The code graph is fully live as a read capability and correctly governed, but it is the most commoditized capability in AI coding and the pilot merchant never touches it, so position it as substrate, never as differentiation. The read path is real: shallow-clone to tree-sitter 15-language parse (off-the-shelf `tree-sitter-language-pack`) to SHA256-incremental to batched pgvector embeddings (`orchestrator/modules/codegraph/codegraph_service.py:121-433,916-1011`) across five workspace-scoped NOT-NULL tables, with all six PRD-165 tools promoted and delivered to every agent through the single `get_tools_for_agent` source (`orchestrator/modules/tools/discovery/actions_codegraph.py:24-152`; `tool_router.py:457-470`) under fail-closed permission checks and staleness stamps. But webhook reindex can never fire because `auto_reindex` has no setter and is only ever read (F022, confirmed; `orchestrator/api/codegraph.py:683`), there is no agent-side write/reindex tool (F087, confirmed), and mission planning never consults code structure — the planning pack is planning-knowledge, planning-history, business-graph, and agent-roster only (`orchestrator/modules/context/modes.py:141-149`). Against Cursor's Merkle-incremental index, Copilot Enterprise, Sourcegraph Cody (product term literally "code graph"), and Greptile's working push-reindex, the dead webhook puts Automatos *behind* the commodity freshness bar. Its value is substrate for the PRD-170 code canvas and proof the governed spine absorbs a whole subsystem; the correct one-quarter goal is reaching that commodity bar (an `auto_reindex` setter, a working webhook, agent index/reindex tools) plus a codegraph slice in the planning pack — not a moat claim.

### Shared field memory — competent engineering, compounding still aspiration

Field memory is live on the write side and genuinely reaches agent turns, but the compounding claim that makes it interesting is structurally blocked, so it is competent plumbing whose superiority over plain context-passing has never been evidenced. The live path is real: a single Qdrant `field_memory` collection with a `workspace_id` payload index bootstrapped at startup (`orchestrator/core/boot/startup_tasks.py:66-83`), a provenance-carrying inject on task completion (`orchestrator/services/coordinator_service.py:630-655`), merge-at-terminal into the workspace field (`orchestrator/modules/context/adapters/vector_field.py:371`), hourly compaction, a dispatch digest verifiably landing in the dispatched agent's prompt (`orchestrator/modules/coordination/dispatcher.py:900-902`), and the workspace field feeding semantic agent matching (`orchestrator/modules/coordination/match_signals.py:140-146`) — all governed by one pure config-driven scoring module (`orchestrator/modules/context/field_scoring.py:28-109`). The blockers matter: the digest queries only the near-empty per-mission field, planning has no field section (`modes.py:141-149`), a running mission shadows workspace recall (F021, confirmed; `platform_executor.py:842-856`), and compaction hard-deletes with no field-to-durable promotion (`vector_field.py:390-445`), so the workspace field barely reaches new missions. The PRD-108 A/B harness that would prove the field beats plain context-passing exists with zero importers (`orchestrator/modules/context/experiment.py`). On novelty, the three-factor score is Generative Agents' recency-importance-relevance plus MemoryBank's Ebbinghaus decay, tenant-scoped graph memory is the established Zep/Graphiti (arXiv:2501.13956) and Mem0g (arXiv:2504.19413) category, and the durable layer is itself a mem0-graph fork. Defensibility within a quarter: promote field to durable before compaction prunes, add a budgeted workspace-field slice to planning and dispatch, fix the F021 shadowing, then run the harness for the PRD's own retention number — without which the field is unfalsifiable plumbing.

### The investor-safe moat paragraph

The honest investor line: Automatos does not claim novel algorithms — semantic tool search is in Anthropic's own API, and the learned-edge design has published lineage in ToolNet and Zep. What is built, live and CI-green in production, is the layer the labs do not give you: a multi-tenant execution substrate where every agent action — chat, voice, a paying merchant's storefront widget, scheduled missions — runs through one governed, permission-fail-closed spine, and every execution is logged and compiled nightly into per-workspace graphs. There is a commerce knowledge graph already grounding a live merchant concierge for the INBUILD pilot, a code graph every agent can query, and a tool-execution graph with success-weighted edges that already shape the default live chat tool list. The moat is not the mechanism; it is the accumulation of per-tenant operating graphs — which tools work, in what order, for which intents, with what revenue outcome — that a competitor cannot bootstrap from zero, that Shopify will not build because it is each merchant's private operational data, and that a single-operator tool like OpenClaw structurally cannot hold with its flat tool lists, markdown-and-SQLite memory, and no tenancy. The write side of that flywheel is live today; the read side that would make it a proven per-tenant asset — workspace-scoped edge reads, the Composio surface joining the graph, planning consuming the field, published evals — is this quarter's roadmap, and the exact diffs are named and small. Told that way, and only that way, the graph-native moat is investor-safe: one genuine open lane, a quarter of targeted work from defensible, with no algorithm the platform pretends to have invented.

## 9. Agent architecture vs Claude Code - the agentic loop

The prior review's headline worry - that Automatos does not really run an agentic loop - is half wrong, and wrong in the good direction: a genuine, model-driven loop exists and is live, but only one of the platform's execution surfaces is actually running on it. Chat drives a real loop; every autonomous path - board tasks, mission tasks, scheduled tasks, webhooks, inter-agent delegation, and the inbound-channel ingest pipeline itself - delivers work into an engine that crashes on its first turn and lies about the result. So the honest verdict is not "no loop"; it is "one loop, one live consumer, and a spine that reports success on failure." That single fact reframes this whole section: the hard part (a correct loop, OS-shaped) is already built, and the gap between "Auto operates the platform" and reality is closeable in hours, not quarters.

### 9.1 Does any path run a real loop? Yes - chat - and here is the evidence

The loop lives in `ToolLoopExecutor` (`orchestrator/modules/tools/execution/tool_loop.py:203-263`). It is not a wrapper around a single completion; it is the genuine article: `while _has_tool_calls(current) and iteration < max_iterations`, it executes the round, appends the assistant tool-call message plus one matching tool-result message per call (`tool_loop.py:249-250`), then re-invokes the model with the grown transcript (`current = await self._llm(messages, tools)`, `tool_loop.py:263`). The model decides each step; the harness owns the transcript and the iteration budget. This is exactly the Claude Code loop shape - send, inspect tool-use, execute, append results verbatim, re-invoke - and the executor already carries the robustness furniture that separates a real agent from a demo: a stuck-loop breaker for repeated identical actions (`tool_loop.py:213-221`), `finish_reason=length` malformed-arguments recovery (`tool_loop.py:223-233`), a dedup and per-tool-call-cap tracker (`tool_loop.py:340`), and - critically - tool exceptions surfaced back to the model as tool content rather than swallowed (`tool_loop.py:363-367`). Anthropic's `stop_reason` and `tool_use` blocks are normalized into this OpenAI-shaped envelope at `orchestrator/core/llm/clients/anthropic_client.py:183-210`. Chat reaches it live: `orchestrator/consumers/chatbot/service.py:2054` makes the initial call, `service.py:2070-2077` enters `_stream_tool_loop`, and the executor is constructed at `service.py:1453`. Iteration caps are already policy rather than code - DB-backed `system_settings` (`orchestrator/config.py:322-366`), seeded with chat at 25. Voice and widget inherit the same executor by construction, so three surfaces share one correct loop.

That is a strength, and it is the load-bearing one for the entire OS thesis. But it only counts as spine-level coherence if the *other* surfaces join it, and they do not.

### 9.2 Where the loop is dead - and why it shipped green

The board task path is severed. `orchestrator/api/board_tasks.py:893` calls `AgentFactory.execute_with_prompt`, which after its first LLM response unconditionally constructs `ToolLoopExecutor(content_truncate_chars=0)` (`orchestrator/modules/agents/factory/agent_factory.py:1066-1071`) - passing a keyword argument that PRD-157 renamed to `content_truncate_tokens` (`tool_loop.py:148-156`). Every call therefore raises `TypeError`, which is caught by the factory's broad retry `except` (`agent_factory.py:1201-1205`), retried with backoff - burning the LLM budget on each attempt - and finally returned as `{"status": "error"}` (`agent_factory.py:1210-1212`). This is finding **F001** (CONFIRMED, critical). The blast radius is every headless surface: board tasks (`board_tasks.py:893`), mission tasks (`orchestrator/services/coordinator_service.py:2001`), scheduled tasks (`orchestrator/services/scheduled_task_service.py:401`), webhooks (`orchestrator/api/webhooks.py:636`), inter-agent delegation (`orchestrator/modules/agents/communication/inter_agent.py:719,792`), and the inbound-channel ingest itself (`orchestrator/channels/base.py:170`). PRD-161 built a genuinely correct exactly-once dispatch spine (finding M5) that faithfully claims each task and delivers it - into a dead engine.

It is worse than a crash, because the board never reads the failure. `board_tasks.py:900-921` never branches on `exec_result["status"]`; it falls through the result-key chain to `""` and marks the task **`done` with `result=None`** (finding **F023**, CONFIRMED, high). The honest-failure branch that PRD-161 wrote (`board_tasks.py:931-957`) is unreachable for any in-band failure. So the platform's most visible claim of autonomy - the board completing work - is currently a green checkmark over nothing. It shipped this way because no test constructs the executor through `execute_with_prompt`: the tests either mock the factory (`tests/test_channel_adapter_contract.py:801`) or exercise the loop class directly (`tests/test_tool_loop_converged.py`), so the constructor contract drift fell into the gap between them.

Two more loops diverge from the good one. The **recipe step** runs a genuine but parallel loop (`orchestrator/api/recipe_executor.py:455+`): model-driven, with cancellation checks, empty-completion retry and fallback-model swap - but no stuck detector, no dedup, no length recovery, no hook points, and raw character truncation (`[:8000]`/`[:20000]`, `recipe_executor.py:687,694`) where chat uses token budgets. **Heartbeats** are a third variant (`orchestrator/services/heartbeat_service.py:699-756`) calling `PlatformActionExecutor.execute` directly with a hardcoded `[:2000]` result cut. The net picture is a correct engine, one live consumer, one crashed consumer that the spine reports as successful, and two half-loops that will drift further apart every release. That is a coherence failure, not a feature gap.

### 9.3 The buildable target - one loop, a policy plane around it

The recommendation is unambiguous: keep `ToolLoopExecutor` as the single loop and do not write a new one. It already matches the target shape; the work is to route every surface into it and wrap it in a policy plane. Section 12 shows this in context as Ring 0 ("one loop, every capability a tool") sitting inside Ring 2 (the policy plane); this section specifies the loop and policy mechanics, and cross-references section 12 for how they compose with memory, routing, and the Jarvis layer rather than duplicating that diagram.

**A policy plane as a new `modules/policy/` package.** This is the single most transferable Claude Code pattern: hooks are policy-as-code that lives *outside* the model, so the model cannot talk its way past them. Introduce a typed event bus (`RunStart`, `PreToolUse`, `PostToolUse`, `PostToolBatch`, `RoundEnd`, `RunEnd`, `PreCompact`) whose handlers return a verdict object `{decision: allow|deny|ask|defer, updated_input, injected_context, reason}` - the exact verdict semantics Claude Code uses, re-keyed for tenancy. Today the executor exposes only post-tool hooks (`tool_loop.py:176-182,254`); the pre-tool seam - the natural attach point for blueprint, approval, and budget policy - is missing (F086, ADJUSTED). Add `on_pre_tool` beside the dedup check in `_execute_round` (`tool_loop.py:340`).

**One cross-surface chokepoint.** Route every tool through `PolicyGate.check()` invoked from `UnifiedToolExecutor.execute_tool` (`orchestrator/modules/tools/execution/unified_executor.py:243+`), where platform, workspace, Composio per-action, and registry tools already converge. The problem today is that the deterministic gate stack - super-admin fail-closed, autonomy dial, confirmation, hierarchy, and a destructive backstop, all in `orchestrator/modules/tools/discovery/platform_executor.py:606-812` - guards `platform_*` actions only; Composio, workspace, and registry tools route around it (`unified_executor.py:334-364`). Refactor that stack into shared `PolicyGate` functions called from both paths so guardrails become universal rather than platform-tool-specific. This directly fixes the governance asymmetry in finding **F060** (CONFIRMED): budget and approval gates exist for missions but not for board tasks or playbooks.

**Parallel tool calls.** All three loops currently run a round's calls sequentially (`tool_loop.py:326`, `recipe_executor.py:534`, `heartbeat_service.py:726`). Group each round by a `parallel_safe` registry flag - `permission_level=='read'` implies parallel-safe - `asyncio.gather` the safe group, serialize the mutating ones, and append all results as one round before the next LLM call. That last clause is a wire-format invariant, not a UX nicety: splitting tool results across messages silently trains the model to stop parallelizing. Fire `PostToolBatch` after the gather, and while here fix the collision-prone id fallback `call_{ms-timestamp}` to a uuid (`tool_loop.py:462-463`).

**Structured error recovery, caps-as-policy.** The error envelope half-exists: `execute_and_format` already returns `success/llm_context/fatal_error/error_type` and relays confirmation stops so Auto can ask instead of erroring (`orchestrator/consumers/chatbot/tool_router.py:715-736`). Formalize it to `{code, message_for_model, remediation, retryable}` and have every policy denial return its reason as tool content - errors-as-data. The DB-settings pattern for caps is already correct; extend it to the stragglers (the hardcoded synthesis cap at `service.py:1415`, the `_MULTI_STEP_TOOLS` name allow-list at `service.py:1259-1264`, and the recipe and heartbeat character cuts) and add the missing pre-call dollar admission inside `on_pre_tool`. Do the dollar accounting model-aware, not flat-rate: F059 (ADJUSTED) confirms four independent hardcoded price tables coexist with an unused model-aware DB registry, so today's approval-budget primitive approves or blocks on wrong dollars.

**Permission modes as act-vs-ask, mapped onto existing primitives - not invented.** `plan` filters tools to `permission_level=='read'` (which also fixes `service.py:807`, where plan mode strips *all* tools so Auto cannot even research while planning); `default` routes `requires_confirmation` to **ask** - the PRD-163 in-chat approval card for chat, an approval row for headless; `auto` is the existing full-autonomy dial (`platform_executor.py:626`), which correctly never satisfies the super-admin gate and never bypasses the destructive backstop; `dontAsk` auto-denies anything unapproved, the right default for webhooks and scheduled runs. Two known gates need fixing here: the admin no-op (F014, CONFIRMED) where `caller_context=None` lets admin-only actions pass whenever the workspace has any owner - true for every workspace - must be replaced with an explicit, default-off "agents inherit admin" policy threading the PRD-168 actor identity; and the rate limiter (`platform_executor.py:794`), the stack's only fail-open gate, must fail closed.

**Prerequisite gates, subagents, skills, MCP.** Enforce preconditions in the tool layer, harness-refuses-until-X rather than prompt-remembers-X: read-before-write on documents and records, plan-approved-before-dispatch (PRD-163 already makes approval a state transition; extend it to destructive board Run-Now), and integration-trusted-before-exposed. Subagents should become child runs with their own transcript, *narrower* tool grants (per-agent ACL already enforced at `tool_router.py:265-277`), and result-only return - context isolation doubling as the prompt-injection blast-radius limiter; the delegation path exists but is dead behind the same F001 kwarg bug. Skills already have their substrate from PRD-22 (`orchestrator/api/skills.py`, `skill_loader.py`); adopt progressive disclosure and a `disable_model_invocation` flag for human-only runbooks - noting the skills router itself carries a critical cross-tenant defect (F002) that must be fixed alongside. There is **no MCP client in the orchestrator today** (zero grep hits for `modelcontextprotocol` or `MCPClient`); add MCP as one more tool source behind `UnifiedToolExecutor`, named `mcp__server__tool` so permission rules compose, with a tenant-scoped server registry, an admin trust gate (a workspace's own content can never approve its own servers), vaulted per-tenant OAuth, output-token caps, and outputs treated as untrusted input.

### 9.4 What does NOT transfer from Claude Code - and what replaces it

Claude Code is a single-user harness on a trusted machine; several of its primitives are actively dangerous in multi-tenant SaaS, and each needs a deliberate replacement rather than a port.

| Claude Code primitive | Why it does not transfer | Replacement in Automatos |
|---|---|---|
| `bypassPermissions` / skip-prompts | Justified only by "isolated VM, can't damage the host"; no tenant-safe equivalent | The autonomy dial bounded by non-overridable deny rules + super-admin gate + destructive backstop - the shape already exists at `platform_executor.py:606,626,796` |
| Model sees every tool schema, gated at call time | Fine for one user; wasteful and leaky across tenants | Pre-filter at surface time (per-agent ACL `tool_router.py:265-277` already does this); reserve call-time gates for ask/conditional verdicts |
| Local trust roots (`~/.claude`, repo `.claude/`) | Assumes the machine owner is the authority; a repo can grant itself permissions | Config authority moves to the tenant control plane - DB rows with provenance; tenant content cannot self-grant |
| Hooks as arbitrary shell commands | Remote code execution by configuration, server-side | Keep the event taxonomy and verdict semantics verbatim; execution becomes in-process handlers plus signed webhooks |
| Bash string-prefix permission matching | The docs themselves call it fragile | The typed-tool registry with `permission_level` metadata; `workspace_exec` (the Bash analog) needs a sandbox-first posture, not string rules |
| Session-local "don't ask again" | Ephemeral, unauditable | Durable, auditable, revocable approval grants with scope and expiry (extend PRD-163 approval rows) |
| CLAUDE.md-as-policy | Memory shapes what the model *tries*, never what is *allowed*; compaction erases transcript text | Blueprints and prompts shape intent; enforcement lives only in the policy plane |
| Single-user credentials | No tenant isolation | Per-tenant vaulted, scoped, rotated secrets injected at the execution boundary - the Composio connected-accounts-per-workspace pattern already models this |

The through-line is that Claude Code separates model *intent* from *authorization* and puts authorization in deterministic out-of-process code. Automatos must keep that separation and move the authority from the local filesystem to the tenant control plane. Auto's blueprint tells it what to attempt; the policy plane, and only the policy plane, decides what executes.

### 9.5 Migration order - no big-bang

The sequencing matters because it restores coherence earliest for the least cost, and never lets chat, recipes, and board run different loop semantics for more than one PR at a time.

1. **Resurrect headless execution (hours).** Rename `content_truncate_chars` to `content_truncate_tokens` (`agent_factory.py:1070`); add the missing test that runs `execute_with_prompt` through its real constructor with a stubbed LLM; branch on `exec_result["status"]` at `board_tasks.py:900-921` (mark `failed` with the error text, exactly as missions already do). This one change flips "Auto operates the platform" from false to true at zero architectural cost. Also repoint the stuck-loop learning sink, which currently imports a nonexistent `modules.memory.task_learning` (`tool_loop.py:55`; the real sink is `modules/memory/tool_outcome_capture.py`).
2. **One policy chokepoint.** Extract the platform gate stack into `modules/policy/` `PolicyGate`; invoke it from `UnifiedToolExecutor.execute_tool` so Composio, workspace, and registry tools stop bypassing; make the rate limiter fail closed; put the owner-fallback behind an explicit default-off setting. This ships independently and all three loops inherit universal guardrails on their platform paths immediately.
3. **Loop convergence.** Re-route recipe `_execute_step` onto `ToolLoopExecutor` (cancellation via `on_round_end`, empty-completion retry inside its `llm_callback`, char cuts replaced by token budgets), then fold heartbeat in the same way - one path per PR, behind the PRD-155 contract-test net.
4. **Policy plane v1.** The `on_pre_tool` seam, the deny-to-ask-to-allow rule engine, dollar-budget admission, `ask` wired to PRD-163 approval cards, and the plan-mode read-only fix.
5. **Loop quality.** Parallel rounds via `parallel_safe` plus `PostToolBatch`, exhaustive `stop_reason` normalization (refusal and pause-turn equivalents) at `anthropic_client.py:183+`, and true token streaming of the final synthesis (today `service.py:2105` re-chunks an already-completed string).
6. **Reach.** Subagent child-runs over the revived delegation path, skills progressive disclosure, and the MCP client adapter behind the chokepoint with trust gates.

The rationale is strict: step 1 restores the OS thesis for hours of work; step 2 makes guardrails universal *before* more surfaces join, so nothing new enters ungoverned; step 3 means the hooks built in step 4 are built once, on a converged spine, rather than three times. Everything after is additive. The reassuring finding for a founder who asked for truth is that the expensive, genuinely-hard artifact - a correct, robust, model-driven loop with OS-shaped extension points - already exists and is proven live in chat; the remaining work is welding the other surfaces onto it and wrapping it in policy, and the critical first weld is measured in hours.

## 11. Competitive benchmark

On the two axes that actually differentiate an agent platform, Automatos is on the right side of both; on almost every axis that governs distribution it is losing today. That is the whole finding — the moat is defensible and the go-to-market is not — and the two must be held together or the benchmark misleads. Competitor claims were re-verified live on 2026-07-01; moat *novelty* is settled in section 8.

### 11.1 The two axes that matter, and the vanity axis that does not

Tool counts are a vanity metric and should never anchor a pitch: Composio hands Automatos roughly 856 apps, Zapier exposes 8,000–9,000 apps and 30,000-plus MCP actions to any LLM (zapier.com/mcp), Lindy advertises 6,000-plus integrations (lindy.ai) — everyone has tools. The axes on which this platform can be honestly compared are **graph-native versus flat-list-plus-static-config** and **depth on a real vertical versus breadth of connector counts**, and against OpenClaw — the field's distribution phenomenon and most instructive comparator — Automatos leads on both. OpenClaw's tool surface is a flat list in the system prompt every turn and its memory is markdown plus SQLite; deepwiki confirmed live on 2026-07-01 that it "does not currently feature a knowledge graph or learned/graph-based tool selection." Automatos ships a live operating graph and a commerce knowledge graph wired end-to-end and deployed on a paying UK merchant, inbuilduk.com (`docs/HANDOFF-2026-05-26.md:60-71`), both scored below with their confirmed limits. OpenClaw has no vertical at all, and Sierra's trajectory ($15.8B on a $950M Series E, $150M-plus ARR, more than 40% of the Fortune 50; cnbc.com, 2026-05-04) is the field's proof that owned vertical outcomes beat connector counts.

### 11.2 The axes scored honestly

On axis (a), the operating graph is real, live, and confirmed by section 8 as the platform's one real moat *candidate* — but the *mechanism* is commodity and must never be pitched as invention. Section 8 settles novelty against Anthropic's first-party Tool Search Tool (Opus 4.5 MCP-eval 79.5% to 88.1%, anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use), OSS equivalents (langgraph-bigtool, RAG-MCP arXiv:2505.03275), and prior art in ToolNet (arXiv:2403.00839, March 2024), a trajectory-edge tool graph re-weighted by an evaluator two years early. What nobody commercial visibly ships is a persistent, **per-tenant, outcome-labeled** graph driving production routing — that is the real position, the loop closing in production while the labs treat search strategy as a pluggable seam. Two confirmed defects temper it: the Composio half of selection is still lexical SQL ILIKE and telemetry collapses the whole 856-app surface to one graph node (F016), so the platform does not yet practice its own thesis externally; and the non-chat agent path is broken on main (F001, critical), erroring on every board task, mission and scheduled run and starving the flywheel of the autonomous trajectories the moat needs. It is a data flywheel fed almost entirely by chat.

On axis (b), the commerce-graph chain is real code (`modules/knowledge/graph_extraction.py:503,693` feeding a per-workspace graph the storefront widget walks for cross-sell), but three confirmed gaps keep it short of a moat: the mappers have zero tests and the headline journey is skipped (F091); catalog webhooks are a silent no-op so the "live" graph is manual-refresh-only (F032); and the `/sync` endpoints are unauthenticated (F003, critical). Meanwhile Shopify is platformizing the catalog itself — Catalog and Storefront MCP let any agent "search across hundreds of millions of Shopify listings," with UCP co-developed with Google (shopify.dev/docs/agents), so the catalog leg is being given away by the platform owner. The defensible thing, as section 8 concludes, is the join Shopify will not make: a per-tenant operational graph of orders, customers and campaigns fused with execution outcomes and memory — within roughly one PRD of real.

### 11.3 Wins and losses

Automatos **wins** on four criteria, each a category OpenClaw's single-operator architecture cannot enter without a rearchitect. On **tenancy and RBAC**, OpenClaw's own docs call session identifiers "routing selectors, not authorization tokens" and prescribe separate gateways per user, whereas Automatos runs one hybrid auth dependency across roughly 745 endpoints with fail-closed observability routers. On **graph-native memory and routing**, the fusion of knowledge graph, code graph, field memory and learned-edge routing has no cohort equivalent (section 8). On **governance as policy**, it has confirmation gates, write/destructive rate limits at the execution choke point and an approval-policy engine with dollar ceilings (PRD-163, merged), against OpenClaw's pairing codes and sandbox flags. On **vertical depth**, it has a live commerce graph on a paying pilot the cohort lacks.

The **losses** are more numerous and every one is distribution-shaped.

### 11.4 What to copy, and how to leapfrog

Five features are worth copying: OpenClaw's **skills-as-markdown progressive disclosure** (body loaded on demand, matching the operating-graph load policy); **pairing-by-default, DM allowlists, and a security-audit command** as per-tenant defaults; **ACP-style wrapping** of external coding harnesses as persistent sessions (relevant to the PRD-170 code canvas); a **bring-your-own-subscription or local-model cost lever** to blunt the "free OpenClaw" objection; and the ClawHavoc **marketplace anti-lesson** — 341 malicious skills, 11.9% of the registry including the top-ranked one (koi.ai, thehackernews.com) — meaning any Automatos registry needs signing, publisher identity and sandboxed install on day one.

To leapfrog: **ship and measure** the per-tenant outcome-labeled operating graph and publish a routing-uplift benchmark against the public yardsticks (the Opus 4.5 88.1% figure) — if learned edges add fewer than five to ten points, kill the claim before a diligence room does; this is gated on fixing F001 and extending edges over the lexical Composio path. Then **pitch the join, not the catalog**; lead with **organizational memory** OpenClaw's per-agent markdown cannot match; own the **governed multi-tenant agent OS for teams** that Sierra (not self-hostable) and OpenClaw (single-operator) both leave open; and **drop the "agent OS" label** publicly (AIOS owns it academically, arXiv:2403.16971; Sierra commercially). The two threats to watch are an OpenClaw Foundation shipping orgs and RBAC, and Composio adding outcome learning to its Tool Router — the defence against both is to own the edge data above the tool backend and keep it portable, investing in the per-tenant graphs and governed board/mission surface, not loop plumbing the Claude Agent SDK already sells.

**Bottom line.** The field cannot quickly copy per-tenant learned edges fused with tenancy and a commerce operational graph; Automatos cannot quickly copy 381k stars. The move is to never compete for the free personal-assistant market, close the four confirmed honesty gaps (the agent-loop break, the lexical Composio routing, the unauthenticated sync with its skipped journey test, the broken self-host), and make the flywheel measurable before claiming it.

### 12.2 Ring 0 - one loop, every capability a tool

The organizing principle is that exactly one agentic loop should exist in the OS, and every trigger - chat, board claim, mission dispatch, recipe step, heartbeat, webhook - merely constructs it with a mode. That loop already exists and is the expensive, genuinely hard artifact: `ToolLoopExecutor` (`orchestrator/modules/tools/execution/tool_loop.py:128`) is a real model-driven loop with a stuck-loop breaker, malformed-argument recovery, and tool errors surfaced back to the model rather than swallowed. The problem is that only chat is alive on it. Today recipes and heartbeats run divergent inline loops, and every non-chat surface dies at one line: `agent_factory.py:1070` passes `content_truncate_chars` to a constructor that only accepts `content_truncate_tokens` (renamed by PRD-157), raising a `TypeError` that the retry `except` swallows, so board tasks, missions, scheduled runs, webhooks, and inter-agent delegation all burn LLM budget and return `status:error` (F001, confirmed). The seams to weld:

- **Spine to engine.** Exists: `board_dispatcher.py` claim and notify against `agent_factory.py` `execute_with_prompt`. Missing: the F001 kwarg fix, plus checking the returned `status` so error dicts stop closing tasks as `done` with `result=None` (`orchestrator/api/board_tasks.py:900-921`, F023 confirmed). **(S - the single highest-leverage change in the repo.)**
- **Lease heartbeat.** Exists: a one-shot 600-second lease (`board_dispatcher.py:176-205`) against the loop's iteration boundary. Missing: a post-tool-hook lease renewal so runs over ten minutes are not swept into double execution (F024 confirmed); the same hook closes the kanban-drag re-execution path (`board_tasks.py:550-555`, F025 confirmed). **(S)**
- **Recipe and heartbeat fold-in.** Exists: `orchestrator/api/recipe_executor.py` `_execute_step` already borrows chatbot components. Missing: both inline loops replaced by `ToolLoopExecutor(mode=RECIPE|HEARTBEAT)` so recovery, telemetry, and hooks apply uniformly, and the raw character cuts (`recipe_executor.py:687,694`) become token budgets. **(M)**
- **Loop robustness to reference grade.** The Claude Code loop contract - exhaustive `stop_reason` handling including `refusal` and `pause_turn`, and parallel `tool_use` batches returned as one result message - versus today's strictly sequential per-call loop (`tool_loop.py:326`, F086 adjusted). Missing: a batch executor keyed on a `parallel_safe` registry flag plus a stop-reason switch. **(M)**

### 12.4 Ring 2 - the policy plane (guardrails as policy, not code)

- **Generalize the approval engine.** Exists: `orchestrator/core/services/approval_policy.py` (mission-plan only) against the loop call sites. Missing: `PolicyEngine.evaluate(principal, tool, args, projected_spend)` invoked pre-tool for every tool including `composio_execute`, where an ask verdict pauses the run and emits the approval card. **(L - the centerpiece build; in-process and deterministic first, out-of-process later.)**

- **Act-versus-ask surface.** Exists: `frontend/components/widgets/MissionApprovalWidget/index.tsx` against policy verdicts. Missing: generalize the in-chat card to any ask verdict from any surface - chat, channel, or widget. **(S/M)**

## 13. Enterprise-grade, dependency-ordered plan

This plan takes Automatos from nineteen sophisticated capabilities that mostly do not join up to a multi-tenant SaaS-grade operating system with Auto as the orchestrator, buildable by a solo founder driving agent fleets in fourteen PRD-sized waves over roughly eight months, with Wave 1 startable Monday morning in a single afternoon. The ordering follows the one spine that matters — coherence first (make the orchestration engine run at all), then the policy plane and deployability that turn the platform into a product two ways, then the moat that compounds with use, then enterprise hardening, then flagship polish. Every wave item carries a build size (S/M/L), a risk flag, its dependencies, and a one-line test-first acceptance criterion: the failing test written before the code. The waves are fed by the fixes catalogued in Section 4 and the integration seams analysed in Section 12; this section sequences and gates that work rather than restating it.

The coherence verdict governs everything below. On `origin/main` today Auto can converse but cannot orchestrate, because `agent_factory.py:1070` passes the kwarg `content_truncate_chars=0` to a `ToolLoopExecutor` constructor that PRD-157 renamed to accept only `content_truncate_tokens` (`tool_loop.py:154`). Fixing one line makes "Auto operates everything end-to-end" true, the precondition for every wave that follows.

## Appendix A — Verification methodology and refuted claims

The protocol in one paragraph: raw claims from the nineteen-domain audit fan-out were pooled (199 defect claims, plus hardcoded-policy, dump, and missing candidates), de-duplicated to root causes, and then each surviving claim was independently re-verified against the pinned commit `37fdecc4e` — critical claims under a two-plus-one-vote rule, all findings re-checked in per-domain batches with a file:line trace and a verify directive, and the six graph-native moat claims scored through three-lens panels (code-liveness, prior-art novelty, operator-value); a claim was asserted as fact only if it survived as CONFIRMED (the original claim held) or ADJUSTED (the claim held with a corrected scope, severity, or line reference), and every claim that failed — refuted at the headline, downgraded below the reporting bar, or merged into another finding — was resolved inside the funnel rather than carried into the register.
